# Marsha P. Johnson

*1945 – 1992 · Gender identity, poverty, queer rights*

You play a street activist in New York in the 1960s to 1990s with little money, little protection and a lot of friends. Offices and officials rarely help you. The night, the streets and the people there do. Content note: discrimination, poverty, violence and an unresolved death.

## Documented events (what the game is based on)

Every effect on the board points to one of these events. If there is no documented event, there is no effect.

- **e1** (1963): Marsha moved to New York from New Jersey as a teenager with little money and slept in the streets and with friends. *Source: New York Public Library archives; Marsha P. Johnson Institute (marshap.org)*
- **e2** (1960s to 1980s): Police arrested and harassed trans women and drag performers for how they dressed; Marsha was arrested many times. *Source: New York Public Library archives; Marsha P. Johnson Institute (marshap.org)*
- **e3** (1960s to 1970s): Trans women faced almost total job discrimination; Marsha worked as a waitress and in survival sex work. *Source: New York Public Library archives; Marsha P. Johnson Institute (marshap.org)*
- **e4** (1970): In 1970 she and Sylvia Rivera founded STAR; a house for homeless trans youth followed in 1971. *Source: New York Public Library archives; Marsha P. Johnson Institute (marshap.org)*
- **e5** (1975): She performed with the Hot Peaches troupe and was photographed by Andy Warhol. *Source: New York Public Library archives; Marsha P. Johnson Institute (marshap.org)*

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Elizabeth, New Jersey, 1945  
**Story**

Marsha is born on 24 August 1945 in Elizabeth, New Jersey, one of seven children in a churchgoing family. She loves flowers and bright clothes.

### 2. Harassed for her clothes  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

As a child and teenager Marsha is harassed for wearing girls' clothes. After high school in 1963 she moves to New York City with very little money in her pocket, hoping for more room to be herself.

### 3. No home, no safety net  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

In New York Marsha often has no place to sleep. She works as a waitress and in sex work to survive. Landlords and police discriminate against queer and trans people, and there is little help for someone with no address.

### 4. A name and a neighbourhood  
**Boost (+2 fields)**

Marsha adds a "P." to her name. In Greenwich Village she becomes a familiar, kind and funny figure, known for flowers in her hair and for sharing food and money with those who have less.

### 5. What did the P stand for?  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

Marsha used the P in her name as an answer to nosy questions about her gender.

*Question:* What did Marsha say the P in her name stood for?

- ✔ "Pay it no mind"
- "Peace and love"
- "Pride"

*Explanation:* She used "Pay it no mind" as a reply to questions about her gender and identity.

### 6. Stonewall, June 1969  
**Story**

When police raid the Stonewall Inn, a gay bar in the Village, people fight back, and protests continue for days. Marsha was part of the community in those days. Accounts differ on what exactly she did, and the "first brick" story is disputed.

### 7. STAR House, 1970  
**Boost (+2 fields)**

With her friend Sylvia Rivera, Marsha founds STAR, Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries. They rent a building as a shelter for homeless trans and queer youth and pay for it with the money they earn themselves.

### 8. Who founded STAR with Marsha?  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

Marsha did not build STAR House alone. A friend and fellow activist shared the work and the rent.

*Question:* Who co-founded STAR and STAR House with Marsha?

- ✔ Sylvia Rivera
- Audre Lorde
- Harvey Milk

*Explanation:* Sylvia Rivera and Marsha founded STAR in 1970. Both were later left out of much of the mainstream gay rights story.

### 9. Left out by the movement  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Many gay rights groups of the time wanted to look "respectable" and kept trans people, drag queens and sex workers at the edge. Marsha and Sylvia were pushed away from events they helped to start.

### 10. Stage and portraits  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

Marsha performs with the drag troupe Hot Peaches. In 1975 the artist Andy Warhol photographs her for his series "Ladies and Gentlemen". Art and performance give her income, friends and an audience.

### 11. The AIDS crisis  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

In the 1980s many friends die of AIDS while officials and media look away. Marsha is diagnosed with HIV in 1990. She takes part in ACT UP, demanding care, research and respect for the people most affected.

### 12. July 1992 and what remains  
**Story**

Marsha's body is found in the Hudson River in July 1992. Police first called it suicide; her friends did not accept that. The case was later reopened and her death remains unresolved. New York announced a monument for her and Sylvia Rivera in 2019.
