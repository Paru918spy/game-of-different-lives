# Sam (fictional)

*fictional · Non-binary identity, gender stereotypes*

You are Sam, a fictional 15-year-old in Germany who goes by they/them. Playing this life means noticing how many forms, rooms and routines assume only two choices, and how much a single person who gets it right can change.

## Documented events (what the game is based on)

Every effect on the board points to one of these events. If there is no documented event, there is no effect.

- **e1** (2017 to 2018): Germany's Federal Constitutional Court ruled in 2017 that civil status must allow a third entry; 'divers' was introduced in 2018. Before that, forms offered two boxes. *Source: Federal Constitutional Court of Germany, 2017 ruling on a third gender entry; 2018 introduction of "divers"*
- **e2** (research): Young LGBTQ people with at least one accepting adult report better mental health (Trevor Project, a US study). *Source: Trevor Project research on acceptance and wellbeing*
- **e3** (research): Trans and non-binary young people report more bullying and exclusion at school (Trevor Project). *Source: Trevor Project research on acceptance and wellbeing*
- **e4** (typical case): School routines such as sports teams, toilets and trips are often split into two groups. *Source: Trevor Project research on acceptance and wellbeing*

## Routes (zones)

The legend is the same for every figure: each route kind is one area of life. For Sam, these differ from the normal rule (one field = one step):

| Route | Area of life | Effect | Why | Event |

| 🕳 Dark tunnel | Isolation, silence and communication barriers | slow (each field costs 1.5 steps) | School climate: jokes and exclusion at school isolate. | e3 |

## Game balance (not part of the story)

20 balance steps (up to 3 per roll). They exist only so that every figure has about the same chance to win; they are not a claim about this person's life.

## Advantage: Allies in the room

Lift: add 2 to your roll after rolling · 2 uses per game.

Sam's friends and a supportive team make the difference when a form or a classroom only offers two boxes. After rolling, add 2 to your result.

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Sam (fictional character)  
**Story**

Sam is a fictional character. Sam was named Samuel at birth, and "boy" never quite fitted, nor did "girl". At 15 Sam has chosen the name Sam and the pronouns they/them, and has told a few close friends.

### 2. Two boxes only  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

School forms say "male" or "female". Teams and changing rooms split in two. The class register still lists the old name, and substitute teachers read it out loud.

### 3. Mr Okafor and Noor  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

A teacher, Mr Okafor, asks Sam privately which name to use and then simply uses it. Sam's friend Noor says, "I've got you", and sits next to Sam at lunch.

### 4. Jokes in the hallway  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

A few classmates whisper and make jokes. Sam skips PE and eats lunch in the library. It is not the identity that is hard, but the reactions of other people.

### 5. A letter to the family  
**Story**

Telling the family is Sam's own decision, and nobody should be forced to do it. Sam writes a letter for their parents. They are shocked at first, then start to ask questions and read.

### 6. What helps young people  
**Question (+2 fields if right)**

Sam looks up what researchers have found about LGBTQ+ young people and wellbeing, and finds that what family and friends do makes a big difference.

*Question:* Which factor is among the strongest for the wellbeing of LGBTQ+ young people?

- ✔ Being accepted by family and friends
- Keeping the identity secret
- Avoiding school events

*Explanation:* Studies such as those of the Trevor Project find that acceptance and support, using the right name and pronouns, are linked with better mental health.

### 7. "It's just a phase"  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Sam's grandfather says it is only a phase. That stings. Sam's mother calmly keeps using Sam's name in front of him, and does not let the subject drop.

### 8. A youth group  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

Sam finds a local youth group for queer young people. Nobody has to explain themselves there, and Sam laughs more than in months. Some members become close friends.

### 9. A third entry in the law  
**Question (+2 fields if right)**

Sam learns that Germany's Federal Constitutional Court ruled in 2017 that the civil register must allow more than "male" and "female". The law changed in 2018.

*Question:* Which option did Germany add to civil records in 2018?

- ✔ A third entry, "divers"
- A ban on gender entries
- An entry for under-18s only

*Explanation:* Since 2018 the German civil register allows "divers" as a third entry, after a ruling in 2017. Many forms and systems are still not updated.

### 10. Forms and offices  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

At 18 Sam wants documents that match who they are. Many forms still have two options, and every office needs it explained again. Sam learns which rules apply and brings a friend to appointments.

### 11. An apprenticeship that listens  
**Boost (+2 fields)**

In a bike repair apprenticeship, the boss asks about pronouns, puts the right name on the staff list and trains the team. Sam says that it sounds small, but changed how every day feels.

### 12. Just Sam  
**Story**

Sam fixes bikes, bakes and plays music. Sam would like to be seen as a person with a name, not as a box. Sometimes Sam is asked to speak at schools, and most questions are "How can I support my friend?"
