# Helen Keller

*1880 – 1968 · Deaf-blindness, gender, politics*

You live through touch: letters spelled into your palm, raised dots under your fingertips, a teacher's hand in yours. The world is slow to learn your language, but you read, write and argue more than almost anyone around you.

## Documented events (what the game is based on)

Every effect on the board points to one of these events. If there is no documented event, there is no effect.

- **e1** (1882 to 1887): After an illness at 19 months she was deaf and blind; for years she had no shared language with her family, only home signs. *Source: Helen Keller, The Story of My Life (1903)*
- **e2** (1887): Anne Sullivan taught her the word 'water' at the pump and then many more words by finger-spelling. *Source: Helen Keller, The Story of My Life (1903)*
- **e3** (1904): She graduated from Radcliffe College with honours, the first deaf-blind person to earn a BA. *Source: Helen Keller, The Story of My Life (1903)*
- **e4** (1912): When she wrote about socialism, newspaper editors dismissed her views as products of her disability. *Source: American Foundation for the Blind Helen Keller Archive*
- **e5** (1892): After her story 'The Frost King' was accused of plagiarism, she faced an inquiry that left her afraid to write. *Source: Helen Keller, The Story of My Life (1903)*

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Tuscumbia, Alabama, 1880  
**Story**

Helen Keller is born on 27 June 1880. At 19 months an illness leaves her deaf and blind. Her family has no way to share language with a toddler who senses everything through touch and smell.

### 2. A home without a shared language  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Helen makes up around sixty home signs, but nobody can teach her more. Frustration grows, and some relatives suggest an institution. In the 1880s the South has almost no education for deaf-blind children.

### 3. The water pump, 1887  
**Boost (+3 fields)**

Anne Sullivan, a teacher from the Perkins School who is partly blind herself, arrives in March 1887. On 5 April, with water running over one hand and a word spelled into the other, Helen grasps that things have names.

### 4. Learning words  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

Helen now learns quickly, from the manual alphabet to braille and later speech.

*Question:* How did Anne Sullivan first teach Helen words?

- ✔ By spelling the words into her palm
- By drawing pictures for her
- By speaking loudly close to her ear

*Explanation:* Anne used the manual alphabet, spelling words with her fingers into Helen's hand while Helen touched the objects they named.

### 5. The Frost King, 1892  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

At 11, Helen writes a story that closely resembles one by another author. She is questioned by a panel of men at Perkins without Anne present. She had absorbed the tale through hand-spelling and did not remember it.

### 6. Radcliffe College, 1904  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

Helen graduates cum laude from Radcliffe. Anne spells lectures into her hand, and few textbooks exist in braille, so she often depends on helpers. She is the first deaf-blind person in the US to earn a bachelor's degree.

### 7. A writer with opinions  
**Story**

She publishes The Story of My Life in 1903 and goes on to write books and essays. She joins the Socialist Party in 1909 and writes about poverty, factory work and why women need the vote.

### 8. Praised, but not believed  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Newspapers loved her as a symbol, but once she took political positions some editors claimed her ideas came from the people around her. Her reply was that people had praised her until she disagreed with them.

### 9. Beyond the headlines  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

Helen wanted fairness for ordinary people, not only fame.

*Question:* Which organisation did Helen Keller help found in 1920?

- ✔ The American Civil Liberties Union
- The American Red Cross
- The Perkins School for the Blind

*Explanation:* Helen was a co-founder of the ACLU, which defends people's rights, including freedom of speech. She also supported workers' unions and the NAACP.

### 10. Across the world  
**Boost (+2 fields)**

With Anne and later Polly Thomson, Helen lectures in more than 30 countries. She raises money for blind and deaf people and visits soldiers who lost sight in war, helping to open schools and services.

### 11. Anne dies, 1936  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

After almost 50 years together, Anne Sullivan dies. Helen loses her teacher, interpreter and closest friend in a world with very few trained deaf-blind interpreters. Polly Thomson and others carry on the daily work.

### 12. 1968 and what lasts  
**Boost (+2 fields)**

President Johnson gives Helen the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1964. She dies on 1 June 1968, aged 87. The full record shows a campaigner as well as a famous student, and the organisations she backed still run.
