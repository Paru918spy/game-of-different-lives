# Jonas (fictional)

*fictional, aged 58 · Age discrimination*

You play a 58-year-old logistics manager who is suddenly job-hunting after 28 years. The hard part is not what he can do but what employers assume about his age. His experience and his network are real assets.

## Routes (zones)

The shared board has ten kinds of routes. For Jonas, these differ from the normal rule (one field = one step):

| Route | Effect | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🕳 Dark tunnel | slow (each field costs 2 steps) | Online filters and silent rejections: you never learn why your application vanished. |
| 🌳 Quiet lane | fast (each field costs half a step) | 28 years of judgement: Jonas reads a situation and a plan calmly and quickly. |
| 🌉 Stone bridge | fast (each field costs half a step) | Old colleagues and mentors carry him: Mira's tip and Aylin's help matter more than any form. |

All other routes work normally. The stone bridge, the long ramp, the quiet lane and the town hall can never be blocked, so there is always a way.

## Advantage: Seen it before

Free pass: count one question or action as answered right · 3 uses per game.

After decades in logistics Jonas has met most problems before. Once or twice, a question counts as answered right because he has simply done it.

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Monday morning (fictional)  
**Story**

Jonas is an invented character. He is 58, a logistics manager with 28 years in one company, well liked and reliable. Then his company is taken over.

### 2. Redundancy  
**Barrier (-2 fields)**

Jonas is told his position is being cut as part of a restructuring. Younger, cheaper staff stay. He has done nothing wrong.

### 3. The first search  
**Story**

He sends dozens of applications. Many get no reply. At interviews he is asked how he feels about reporting to someone younger.

### 4. "Overqualified"  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Employers assume older workers cost more, adapt less or leave sooner. Jonas has the skills, but the stereotype reaches the desk before he does.

### 5. A word for it  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

What Jonas meets has a name, which the WHO uses in its global report.

*Question:* What is prejudice or discrimination based on age called?

- ✔ Ageism
- Seniority
- Agility

*Explanation:* Ageism means stereotypes and unfair treatment because of age. The WHO's 2021 global report treats it as a widespread problem.

### 6. Online forms and tests  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Long web forms and tests are new to Jonas. Without help he feels lost, and there is no box where 28 years of experience can show.

### 7. A tip from Mira  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

A former colleague, Mira, hears of an opening in a smaller firm and passes it on. Personal networks often count more than the formal process.

### 8. The oldest in class  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Jonas retrains for a new field. Some classmates are friendly. Others assume he will not keep up. Having to prove himself again wears him down.

### 9. Swapping skills with Aylin  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

A young classmate, Aylin, shows him new software. He shows her how to lead a team. Both of them leave the course better off.

### 10. Mixed-age teams  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

Jonas's next team has people from many age groups.

*Question:* What does research usually say about teams of mixed ages?

- ✔ They work well, since each age group brings different strengths
- They always work worse
- Only the youngest members matter

*Explanation:* Studies suggest age-diverse teams do well, because experience and fresh ideas complement each other.

### 11. A new role  
**Boost (+2 fields)**

A company hires Jonas as an operations coordinator. His new manager says his experience is exactly what the team has been missing.

### 12. Not too old  
**Story**

Jonas now trains younger colleagues. He says: "Nobody warned me that you can be treated as too old long before you feel old." He backs hiring that looks at skills, not birth years.
