# Amira (fictional)

*fictional · Refugee experience*

You play a 16-year-old who loves maths and football and has to rebuild her life in a new country. Much of the way is paperwork and waiting that nobody asked her to carry. Her languages, her team spirit and the people who open doors count for a lot.

## Documented events (what the game is based on)

Every effect on the board points to one of these events. If there is no documented event, there is no effect.

- **e1** (typical case): Refugees in Germany often wait months or years for residence decisions, and foreign diplomas have to pass a recognition procedure before they count. *Source: Federal Recognition Act (Anerkennungsgesetz) 2012, anerkennung-in-deutschland.de*
- **e2** (typical case): Newly arrived pupils often sit in class before they speak German; this isolates them even when they are strong in maths or sport. *Source: OECD: Education in Germany, students with a migrant background*
- **e3** (typical case): At the same performance level, pupils with a migrant background are placed in lower school tracks more often (OECD, German school studies). *Source: OECD: Education in Germany, students with a migrant background*
- **e4** (typical case): Sports clubs are one of the most common first places where refugee children find friends; the DOSB has run integration programmes since 1989. *Source: DOSB programme 'Integration durch Sport' (dosb.de)*
- **e5** (typical case): Teachers, volunteers and neighbours are one of the most important factors for refugee children arriving in a new country (UNHCR). *Source: UNHCR reports on refugee education*

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Home (fictional)  
**Story**

Amira is an invented character, based on what many refugees report. She is 16, loves maths and football. Her father teaches, her mother is a nurse. Then war reaches her city and damages the school and the hospital.

### 2. Leaving everything  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Her family decides to leave. They can only take what they can carry. Amira says goodbye to friends, her grandmother and her school books.

### 3. Months on the road  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

The journey takes months and depends on strangers, closed borders and long waits. Amira keeps her little brother calm by telling him stories.

### 4. Kindness at a border camp  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

A volunteer doctor treats her brother. A teacher gives Amira a notebook, so she can keep doing maths. Small acts of help carry a family a long way.

### 5. Arrival in Germany  
**Story**

The family lives in a shared accommodation and waits for months for papers and a school place. Everything is new: the language, the food, the rules, the weather.

### 6. Why can't her parents work?  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

In Germany her father drives a delivery van. Her mother cannot work as a nurse. Why?

*Question:* Why can Amira's parents not work as a teacher and a nurse?

- ✔ Their foreign qualifications are not recognised yet
- They do not want to work
- Refugees are never allowed to work

*Explanation:* Recognising foreign diplomas is slow and complicated in many countries. The skills are there, the system is the barrier.

### 7. A language she doesn't speak yet  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

In class Amira understands little. She is quick at home, but here some classmates see only a girl who cannot speak. A few tease her scarf.

### 8. A teacher and Jana  
**Boost (+3 fields)**

A teacher stays after class to practise German with her. A classmate, Jana, invites her to football. Amira starts to feel she belongs somewhere.

### 9. Waiting for a decision  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

The family waits years for a decision on their status. Plans for study, work and travel stay on hold, because the rules decide, not effort.

### 10. Which school track?  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

A counsellor suggests a shorter, practical path. Amira wants to study medicine. Studies look at this pattern.

*Question:* What do studies find about pupils from migrant families with the same grades?

- ✔ They are more often advised to take lower school tracks
- They are always advised to take the highest track
- Their track advice is identical to everyone else's

*Explanation:* Research on school recommendations in Germany shows a bias against migrant families. Mentors and teachers can help push back.

### 11. Her Abitur  
**Boost (+2 fields)**

With extra tutoring and a mentor, Amira insists on the Abitur and passes it. Her parents are proud, and she is accepted to study medicine.

### 12. Interpreting at the clinic  
**Boost (+2 fields)**

Amira volunteers as an interpreter for new arrivals. She knows how it feels to have nobody who understands you. She now feels at home in two places.
