# Malala Yousafzai

*b. 1997 · Girls’ education, gender*

You play a student whose right to go to school is taken by decree, then defended by family, teachers and strangers on the other side of the world. Rules and checkpoints slow you down, and the people who stand with you speed you up.

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Mingora, 1997  
**Story**

Malala is born on 12 July 1997 in Mingora, in the Swat Valley, Pakistan. Her father, Ziauddin, runs a school, and in her family a daughter's education matters as much as a son's. She loves reading and speaking up in class.

### 2. A family that backs her  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

In many families in the region girls are expected to leave school early. Malala's parents insist she and her brothers study the same. Debating and public speaking in her father's school give her confidence.

### 3. Girls banned from school  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

From 2007 the Pakistani Taliban take control in parts of Swat. They ban music and television and destroy schools. In January 2009 they announce that girls may no longer attend school. Malala is eleven.

### 4. A diary under another name  
**Story**

Malala writes a diary for BBC Urdu about life under the Taliban. To protect her, it appears under a false name. She describes fear, closed classrooms and how much she wants to learn. Her identity becomes known later.

### 5. The pen name  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

Malala's blog for BBC Urdu was published under a name to keep her safe.

*Question:* What pen name did Malala use for her BBC Urdu diary?

- ✔ Gul Makai
- Nur Jahan
- Zeba Khan

*Explanation:* Gul Makai means "cornflower" in Pashto. A BBC reporter chose it to protect her identity.

### 6. A national youth prize  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

After the army pushes the Taliban back, Malala keeps campaigning. In 2011 she receives Pakistan's first National Youth Peace Prize, and she and her father speak up for schools that were destroyed.

### 7. 9 October 2012  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

A gunman boards Malala's school bus and shoots her because she speaks for girls' education. She is 15. She is flown to Birmingham, UK, for surgery and long recovery. Her friends on the bus are also hurt. Content note: violence.

### 8. A voice at the United Nations  
**Boost (+2 fields)**

Messages arrive from all over the world. Malala recovers, goes back to school in England and, on her 16th birthday in July 2013, speaks at the United Nations. With her father she starts the Malala Fund for girls' schooling.

### 9. Youngest laureate  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

In 2014 Malala shares the Nobel Peace Prize with Kailash Satyarthi, an Indian child-rights campaigner.

*Question:* How old was Malala when she won the Nobel Peace Prize?

- ✔ 17
- 21
- 14

*Explanation:* She was 17 in 2014, the youngest Nobel laureate ever.

### 10. Lies and threats online  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Some online accounts claim without evidence that she is a foreign agent or that the attack was staged. She keeps getting threats. Fame means being judged by people who have never met her.

### 11. Home again, and Oxford  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

In 2018 Malala visits Pakistan again for the first time since the attack. In 2020 she graduates from Oxford University in philosophy, politics and economics.

### 12. A pen, a book, a teacher  
**Story**

Malala's fund supports local educators and girls' schooling in countries such as Pakistan, Afghanistan and Nigeria. She says millions of girls are still out of school. Her message: one child, one teacher, one book and one pen can change the world.
