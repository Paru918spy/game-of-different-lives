# Lena (fictional)

*fictional · Disability, accessibility, work*

You play a young woman who uses a wheelchair and wants to be an architect in a small German town. Steps, cobblestones and forms stand in her way, and her eye for space and design is a real skill. Playing her means seeing how much of the world is built for one kind of body.

## Routes (zones)

The shared board has ten kinds of routes. For Lena, these differ from the normal rule (one field = one step):

| Route | Effect | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🪜 Stairway | blocked (this route cannot be taken) | No lift, no ramp: a staircase is a wall. She cannot take this shortcut and must find another way. |
| 🪢 Rope bridge | slow (each field costs 2 steps) | Cobbles and uneven ground jolt a wheelchair. Every metre costs more effort and time. |
| ↗ Long ramp | fast (each field costs half a step) | Lena knows every step-free route in town and uses it without a detour. |
| 🌳 Quiet lane | fast (each field costs half a step) | Drawing and designing are where she shines. Her best ideas come when she can focus on a plan. |

All other routes work normally. The stone bridge, the long ramp, the quiet lane and the town hall can never be blocked, so there is always a way.

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. A small town (fictional)  
**Story**

Lena is an invented character. She is 12, uses a wheelchair after a spinal injury as a child, and loves drawing houses and bridges. The old market square is cobbled, and many shops have steps.

### 2. Which school?  
**Barrier (-3 fields)**

The nearest school has no lift. The special school is an hour away by bus. Her parents fight for months to get a place at the local school with a ramp and support.

### 3. Friends and Ms Berger  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

At her new school classmates quickly stop noticing the chair. Her teacher, Ms Berger, makes sure class trips go to places Lena can reach.

### 4. Left out of the trip  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

In year 9 the trip goes to a hostel with no lift. Lena is told she can "stay home or be carried". Her friends refuse to go until the plans change.

### 5. Who else uses a ramp?  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

Lena notices that step-free access helps more people than just wheelchair users.

*Question:* Which group also benefits from a step-free entrance?

- ✔ Parents with prams and people with heavy luggage
- Nobody else
- Only people who run marathons

*Explanation:* Step-free design helps parents, older people, cyclists and delivery staff. Many accessible features are useful for everyone.

### 6. Forms for university  
**Barrier (-3 fields)**

Lena applies to study architecture. Some lecture halls and studios are not accessible. Support and technical aids mean many forms and months of waiting.

### 7. A flat of her own  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

After 40 viewings, many landlords saying "it isn't suitable" before she even visits, Lena finds a small accessible flat near the university.

### 8. The professor's question  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

A professor asks whether she can do site visits. She answers with a plan: site photos, drones, a partner on site.

*Question:* What is the main problem on an inaccessible building site?

- ✔ Missing ramps and step-free access
- The wheelchair itself
- Lena's ability to draw

*Explanation:* The barrier is the missing access, not the person. Flexible solutions and adapted workplaces help everyone.

### 9. Her degree  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

Lena graduates with a design for an accessible community centre. Her professor calls it the best of the year.

### 10. Interviews  
**Barrier (-2 fields)**

At job interviews Lena is asked about her "limitations" and the cost of adapting the office. Several offices have no step-free entrance.

### 11. An inclusive firm  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

A mid-sized firm hires Lena and adapts her workspace within two weeks. Wider doors and lower lift buttons help the whole team.

### 12. Designing for everyone  
**Story**

Lena helps plan a market square with level paths, then opens her own studio for accessible design. She says: "I'm not stuck in a wheelchair. I'm stuck with stairs."
