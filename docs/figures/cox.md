# Laverne Cox

*b. 1972 · Gender identity*

You are Laverne Cox: a dancer first, then an actor, producer and advocate. Playing this life means moving between stages where you are celebrated and rooms where gatekeepers and strangers decide who you are allowed to be.

## Routes (zones)

The shared board has ten kinds of routes. For Laverne, these differ from the normal rule (one field = one step):

| Route | Effect | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🪢 Rope bridge | fast (each field costs half a step) | Years of dance training: balance and body control make the swaying bridge feel like a stage. |
| 🌉 Stone bridge | fast (each field costs half a step) | Her mother, friends and other trans artists carry her across, like the bridge itself. |
| 🏛 Town hall | slow (each field costs 2 steps) | Casting offices and paperwork: gatekeepers who would not cast or name her correctly cost time. |
| 🎪 Busy market | slow (each field costs 2 steps) | In public, stares, misgendering and harassment turn a crowded street into hard work. |

All other routes work normally. The stone bridge, the long ramp, the quiet lane and the town hall can never be blocked, so there is always a way.

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Mobile, Alabama, 1972  
**Story**

Laverne is born on 29 May 1972 and raised by her mother, Gloria, together with her twin brother. She loves to dance and perform, and knows from early on that she is a girl.

### 2. Told to fit a role  
**Barrier (-3 fields)**

Classmates and some adults bully Laverne for not acting the way they expect a boy to act. She learns to hide, and has spoken openly about how hard this was for her mental health.

### 3. Dance and a home in the arts  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

Her mother backs her. Laverne attends the Alabama School of Fine Arts, where she studies dance and finds a place where creative kids are taken seriously.

### 4. Studying in New York  
**Question (+2 fields if right)**

Laverne studies at Indiana University and then graduates from Marymount Manhattan College in New York. In her twenties she transitions and builds a community.

*Question:* Which art form did Laverne study at school and at college?

- ✔ Dance
- Architecture
- Journalism

*Explanation:* Laverne trained as a dancer at the Alabama School of Fine Arts and graduated in dance from Marymount Manhattan College.

### 5. No roles, many closed doors  
**Barrier (-3 fields)**

In the 1990s and 2000s there are almost no parts for openly trans actors. Casting people reject her, money is tight, and some employers treat her as a risk or a joke.

### 6. Television and teaching  
**Story**

In 2008 she appears on the reality show I Want to Work for Diddy and then produces and hosts TransAmerican Love Story. She starts using television to explain trans lives to people who have never met a trans person.

### 7. Sophia Burset  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

In 2013 she plays Sophia Burset in Orange Is the New Black, a trans woman played by a trans actress. The Netflix series is a worldwide success and brings many more people to her work.

### 8. A first for acting  
**Question (+2 fields if right)**

In 2014 Time puts Laverne on its cover for the story "The Transgender Tipping Point". The same year her work for Orange Is the New Black earns an Emmy nomination.

*Question:* What was historic about Laverne's 2014 Emmy nomination?

- ✔ First openly trans person nominated for an acting Emmy
- First time a Netflix show was nominated
- First Emmy won by a dancer

*Explanation:* She was the first openly transgender person nominated for an acting Emmy. She also became the first openly trans person on the cover of Time.

### 9. Fame is not safety  
**Barrier (-2 fields)**

Even as a star, Laverne is misgendered, threatened online and drawn into public debates about her identity. She points out that many trans women, especially Black trans women, face this with no platform and no protection.

### 10. Disclosure  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

She uses her platform on purpose. In 2020 she is an executive producer of the documentary Disclosure, in which trans people look at how film and television have portrayed them for a century.

### 11. A lead on network television  
**Story**

In 2017 she stars in the CBS legal drama Doubt, the first openly trans actor in a leading series role on US network television. She keeps working in film, theatre and production.

### 12. Telling your own story  
**Story**

Laverne keeps acting and speaking out. A theme in her work is that trans people should tell their own stories, on screen and behind the camera, instead of having others tell them.
