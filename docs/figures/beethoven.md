# Ludwig van Beethoven

*1770 – 1827 · Deafness*

Music is your language, and you hear it more and more from the inside. You live in Vienna among patrons, publishers and friends, in a world built for listeners, and you have to rework how you compose, talk and earn a living.

## Documented events (what the game is based on)

Every effect on the board points to one of these events. If there is no documented event, there is no effect.

- **e1** (1802): In the Heiligenstadt Testament he wrote that his hearing had been failing for years and that it had pushed him to withdraw from people. *Source: Beethoven-Haus Bonn (beethoven.de)*
- **e2** (from about 1818): He used conversation books: visitors wrote their side of a talk, he answered aloud. *Source: Beethoven-Haus Bonn (beethoven.de)*
- **e3** (1814): His last public appearance as a pianist in a new work was in 1814; after that his hearing no longer let him perform on stage. *Source: Jan Swafford, Beethoven: Anguish and Triumph*
- **e4** (1815 to 1820): After his brother died he fought in the Vienna courts for guardianship of his nephew Karl, a case that cost him years and money. *Source: Jan Swafford, Beethoven: Anguish and Triumph*
- **e5** (1809): Archduke Rudolph and two princes promised him a yearly income so he would stay in Vienna and keep composing. *Source: Jan Swafford, Beethoven: Anguish and Triumph*
- **e6** (1824 to 1826): He kept sketchbooks and heard music in his head; the late quartets and the Ninth were written deaf. *Source: Beethoven-Haus Bonn (beethoven.de)*

## Game balance (not part of the story)

18 balance steps (up to 3 per roll). They exist only so that every figure has about the same chance to win; they are not a claim about this person's life.

## Advantage: The inner ear

Free pass: count one question or action as answered right · 3 uses per game.

Beethoven composed late works from his inner ear and planned whole structures in his head. Up to three times, a question you know well counts as answered.

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Bonn, 1770  
**Story**

Ludwig is baptised in Bonn on 17 December 1770 into a family of court musicians. His father Johann trains him hard on the keyboard, hoping to present him as a child prodigy.

### 2. A household to hold together  
**Barrier (-2 fields)**

Johann drinks, and money is short. After Ludwig's mother dies in 1787 he plays and works as an organist, and he soon becomes the main provider for his younger brothers.

### 3. To Vienna, 1792  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

Ludwig moves to Vienna, studies with Joseph Haydn and is soon admired as a pianist and improviser. Nobles such as Prince Lichnowsky give him rooms, money and a stage.

### 4. The first sign  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

Success is arriving when something starts to change.

*Question:* What did Beethoven first notice around 1798?

- ✔ A ringing in his ears (tinnitus)
- Pain in his hands
- Trouble reading music

*Explanation:* Around 1798, aged 27, he began to hear ringing and buzzing in his ears. His hearing then faded over the following years.

### 5. Hiding it from patrons  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

He fears that a musician who cannot hear will lose patrons, pupils and concerts. So he keeps quiet and avoids parties, which makes him look unfriendly. Nobody around him has any tools for deafness.

### 6. The Heiligenstadt letter, 1802  
**Story**

Content note: despair. In a letter to his brothers that he never sent, Ludwig writes about shame, isolation and thoughts of dying. He says his art held him back, and he went on to compose for 25 more years.

### 7. A new, bolder sound  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

He writes the Third Symphony (the Eroica, 1804), the Fifth with its four-note opening, and the opera Fidelio. Publishers and patrons keep paying for music that sounds like nothing before it.

### 8. New ways to talk  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

By about 1818 he can hardly hear speech at all.

*Question:* How did visitors talk with the almost-deaf Beethoven?

- ✔ They wrote in his conversation books
- They used sign language
- They spoke into a speaking tube

*Explanation:* Friends wrote their half of the conversation in notebooks; he answered aloud. Many of these books survive and show his daily life, and he also tried ear trumpets.

### 9. Walls, courts and debt  
**Barrier (-3 fields)**

Concert halls and salons were designed only for listening. Beethoven also fights for custody of his nephew Karl through the courts from 1815 to 1820, with strained money and health. Few friends can talk with him easily.

### 10. The Ninth, 1824  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

The Ninth Symphony, with its choral Ode to Joy, premieres in Vienna on 7 May 1824. At the end a singer turns Beethoven around so he can see the audience cheering and waving, since he cannot hear the applause.

### 11. The late quartets  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

In his last years he writes string quartets that surprise later composers. They come from what he calls his inner hearing, with friends and copyists who put the score into readable form.

### 12. Vienna, 1827  
**Story**

Beethoven dies on 26 March 1827, aged 56. Many thousands of people line the streets for his funeral. Today he is played everywhere, and the Ode to Joy melody of his Ninth is the anthem of the European Union.
