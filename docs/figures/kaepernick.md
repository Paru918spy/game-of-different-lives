# Colin Kaepernick

*b. 1987 · Race, adoption, protest*

You are the quarterback everyone watches, and often the only Black kid in the room: adopted into a white family, drilled in training, valued for your arm while people assume things about you. When you decide to speak, the whole league can hear it.

## Routes (zones)

The shared board has ten kinds of routes. For Colin, these differ from the normal rule (one field = one step):

| Route | Effect | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🏁 Race track | fast (each field costs half a step) | Years of drills, speed and conditioning: you build your life around training, and running a long track is your home ground. |
| 🎪 Busy market | slow (each field costs 2 steps) | Crowds, boos and cameras: public attention turns every step into a debate, and it costs time. |
| 🏛 Town hall | slow (each field costs 2 steps) | League rules, front offices and lawyers: no team calls, and a grievance takes years to settle. |

All other routes work normally. The stone bridge, the long ramp, the quiet lane and the town hall can never be blocked, so there is always a way.

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Milwaukee, 1987  
**Story**

Colin is born on 3 November 1987 to a white mother and a Black father who is not part of his life. At five weeks he is adopted by Rick and Teresa Kaepernick and grows up in California. He speaks warmly of his adoptive family.

### 2. Hair and being judged  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

As a teen with braids, Colin is pushed by adults to change his hair so he looks “respectable”. He is often the only Black player on his teams, and stereotypes follow him. The series Colin in Black & White dramatises this.

### 3. Few offers, one yes  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Colin shines at baseball and as a quarterback, but gets few scholarship offers to play quarterback. Only the University of Nevada offers him that place.

### 4. To the NFL  
**Boost (+2 fields)**

At Nevada he breaks records, and in 2011 the San Francisco 49ers draft him in the second round. In February 2013 he leads the team to Super Bowl XLVII.

### 5. Watching the news  
**Story**

In 2016 killings of Black men by police dominate the news. Colin reads about them and about American history, and decides he cannot show pride in a flag for a country that, in his view, oppresses Black people.

### 6. Sit, then kneel  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

Colin first sits during the anthem in an August 2016 preseason game. After talking with a former soldier, he changes his way of protesting to something he sees as more respectful.

*Question:* Which gesture did Kaepernick settle on after talking with Nate Boyer?

- ✔ Kneeling during the anthem
- Leaving the stadium
- Raising a banner

*Explanation:* Boyer, a former Green Beret, suggested kneeling as a respectful gesture. The protest spread to other players and divided the country.

### 7. Backlash  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Colin is booed, receives death threats and is called unpatriotic by many. Others, including many players and veterans, support him. He leaves the 49ers after 2016.

### 8. No team calls  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

In 2017 Colin is a free agent, but no NFL team signs him, and he has not played in the league since. He files a grievance accusing the league of collusion, which is settled in 2019.

### 9. Know Your Rights Camp  
**Boost (+3 fields)**

Colin donates large sums and founds a camp for young people that teaches their legal rights, financial literacy and how to handle police encounters.

### 10. What does the camp teach?  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

The camp is built around practical knowledge, not slogans. Young people leave knowing what to do and who to call.

*Question:* What is a main aim of the Know Your Rights Camp?

- ✔ Teaching young people about their legal rights
- Training new quarterbacks
- Selling sports gear

*Explanation:* The camp teaches legal rights, financial literacy and how to act in encounters with police.

### 11. “Believe in something”  
**Boost (+3 fields)**

In 2018 Nike makes Colin the face of a campaign: “Believe in something. Even if it means sacrificing everything.” In 2021 the series Colin in Black & White, co-created with Ava DuVernay, tells his teenage years.

### 12. A conversation that continues  
**Story**

Colin remains a prominent and polarising figure. Some see someone who gave up football for justice; others disagree with the protest. His story asks how much speaking up costs, and who pays.
