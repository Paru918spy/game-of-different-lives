# Judy Heumann

*1947 – 2023 · Disability rights, wheelchair user*

You are Judy, a wheelchair user from Brooklyn who grew up in the 1950s, when schools could simply say no. Steps and locked doors are everywhere, but so are the rules, forms and crowds of people that you will learn to use better than most.

## Routes (zones)

The shared board has ten kinds of routes. For Judy, these differ from the normal rule (one field = one step):

| Route | Effect | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🪜 Stairway | blocked (this route cannot be taken) | Steep steps and no lift: a wheelchair cannot take this stretch at all. |
| 🌳 Tree ladders | blocked (this route cannot be taken) | A tree-house ladder is not usable from a wheelchair. The building, not Judy, left her out. |
| 🏛 Town hall | fast (each field costs half a step) | Judy learned the rules and regulations better than the officials, and used them to win. |
| 🎪 Busy market | fast (each field costs half a step) | Among organised crowds of allies, as at the 1977 sit-in, Judy found support and momentum. |

All other routes work normally. The stone bridge, the long ramp, the quiet lane and the town hall can never be blocked, so there is always a way.

Judy never won with one attempt. She tried again in court, in protest and in Washington until the rules changed. Roll two dice and take the higher one.

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Brooklyn, 1947  
**Story**

Judy is born on 18 December 1947 to German-Jewish parents who had escaped the Nazis. At 18 months she gets polio and begins to use a wheelchair. She grows up in Brooklyn, New York.

### 2. "A fire hazard"  
**Barrier (-3 fields)**

In 1953 her local school refuses to let her attend kindergarten: a wheelchair, it says, is a fire hazard. Her mother Ilse fights the decision, but for years Judy only gets a few hours of home teaching.

### 3. Camp Jened  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

At a summer camp for disabled teenagers in the Catskills, Judy sees a place where disability is normal and decisions are shared. Friendships from camp later grow into activism, and the film Crip Camp tells that story.

### 4. The teaching licence  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

In 1970 New York City refuses Judy a teaching licence because she cannot walk. She takes the city to court.

*Question:* What happened when Judy challenged the city's refusal?

- ✔ She won and became the first wheelchair user to teach in New York City schools
- The court agreed with the city and she gave up teaching
- The city offered her only a desk job without students

*Explanation:* Judy won her case in 1970. The news made her widely known, and she taught in New York City public schools.

### 5. Disabled in Action  
**Story**

In 1970 Judy helps found Disabled in Action, a group led by disabled people. Later she moves to Berkeley, California, a centre of the independent living movement, where disabled people run their own services.

### 6. A law on paper only  
**Barrier (-3 fields)**

Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act (1973) bans discrimination by programmes that get government money. But the government keeps delaying the rules that would make it real, and nothing changes in schools or buildings.

### 7. April 1977  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

Activists across the US demand that the 504 rules be signed. In San Francisco about 150 people occupy a federal building, with no ramps, beds or medicine ready for them.

*Question:* How long did the San Francisco sit-in last?

- ✔ 26 days
- 3 days
- About 6 months

*Explanation:* The sit-in lasted 26 days, the longest occupation of a US federal building at the time. Food came from groups such as the Black Panthers, and a delegation went to Washington.

### 8. Section 504 is signed  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

The 504 regulations are signed. They become the first national civil rights protection for disabled people in the US and the base for the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990.

### 9. Inside government  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

In 1993 Judy becomes Assistant Secretary in the US Department of Education, responsible for special education and rehabilitation. Later she advises the World Bank and the State Department on disability rights worldwide.

### 10. Still not accessible  
**Barrier (-2 fields)**

Even as a senior official Judy meets inaccessible planes, buildings and meeting rooms. She keeps saying that barriers come from choices society makes, not from a person's body.

### 11. Telling the story  
**Story**

Her memoir Being Heumann and the film Crip Camp, both from 2020, bring the movement to a wide public. She is still travelling and speaking when she is in her seventies.

### 12. 2023: nothing about us without us  
**Story**

Judy Heumann dies on 4 March 2023, aged 75. Her principle lives on: disabled people must lead decisions that affect their lives. Many rules that make schools and transport more open began with her movement.
