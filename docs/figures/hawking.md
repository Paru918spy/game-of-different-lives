# Stephen Hawking

*1942 – 2018 · Disability (motor neurone disease)*

You are Stephen, a physicist in Cambridge whose work takes place in your head and on a blackboard, and whose way to the lecture hall is full of stairs. People around you make a lot of your work possible, and some do not wait to hear you finish a sentence.

## Documented events (what the game is based on)

Every effect on the board points to one of these events. If there is no documented event, there is no effect.

- **e1** (1963 to 1964): At 21 he was diagnosed with motor neurone disease (ALS) and told he might have about two years. *Source: Stephen Hawking, A Brief History of Time (1988) and My Brief History (2013)*
- **e2** (late 1960s to 1970s): He moved from sticks to a wheelchair; many Cambridge buildings of the time were not step-free, so he often depended on help to get in. *Source: Stephen Hawking, A Brief History of Time (1988) and My Brief History (2013)*
- **e3** (1985): After pneumonia and a tracheotomy he lost his voice; he first used a spelling card, later a computer with a synthesiser. *Source: Stephen Hawking, A Brief History of Time (1988) and My Brief History (2013)*
- **e4** (1960s to 2018): His wife, nurses, students and colleagues made his daily life and work possible. *Source: Stephen Hawking, A Brief History of Time (1988) and My Brief History (2013)*
- **e5** (1970s): As writing equations became impossible, he trained himself to hold geometry and equations in his head. *Source: Stephen Hawking, A Brief History of Time (1988) and My Brief History (2013)*

## Life stations (in the order of the road)

Every figure must stop at the 12 life stations on the way, one after the other. Effects: barrier = move back, boost = move forward; a question gives its bonus only for a right answer.

### 1. Oxford, 1942  
**Story**

Stephen is born on 8 January 1942 in Oxford to two scientists. At school in St Albans he loves board games and taking things apart, and classmates jokingly call him "Einstein".

### 2. Cambridge, 1962  
**Story**

Stephen starts a PhD in cosmology. In 1963, aged 21, he is diagnosed with motor neurone disease, and doctors expect him to live about two more years. He keeps studying and working.

### 3. Old buildings, no ramps  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

As he starts using sticks and then a wheelchair, Cambridge's old buildings turn into obstacles: steps, narrow doors, no lifts. Everyday tasks need planning and help from others.

### 4. People around him  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

He gets engaged to Jane Wilde and they marry in 1965. Friends, family and fellow students help with daily life, which leaves more of his energy for physics.

### 5. Black holes leak  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

In 1974 Stephen shows that black holes are not completely black. At 32 he becomes one of the youngest Fellows of the Royal Society.

*Question:* What did Stephen show about black holes in 1974?

- ✔ They slowly give off energy, now called Hawking radiation
- They can never form
- They are the same as white dwarf stars

*Explanation:* Hawking radiation means black holes slowly lose energy and can eventually evaporate. It was a surprise to many physicists.

### 6. Newton's old chair  
**Question (+1 fields if right)**

In 1979 Stephen takes up a famous professorship at Cambridge and keeps it for 30 years.

*Question:* Which professorship, once held by Isaac Newton, did Stephen hold?

- ✔ Lucasian Professor of Mathematics
- Astronomer Royal
- Master of Trinity College

*Explanation:* The Lucasian chair of mathematics was created in 1663. Newton held it before Stephen did.

### 7. Geneva, 1985  
**Barrier (-3 fields)**

Severe pneumonia during a trip to Switzerland leads to a tracheotomy, and Stephen can no longer speak. He needs round-the-clock nursing and a new way to communicate, and nobody has built one for him yet.

### 8. A voice from a computer  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

A program called Equalizer lets him choose words with a hand clicker, later with a sensor on his cheek. A speech synthesiser speaks them. He keeps its American-accented voice because it feels like his own.

### 9. Spoken over  
**Barrier (-1 fields)**

Many people who use speech devices report that strangers talk to their carer instead of to them, or assume they understand less. Patience would cost nothing. (A common experience, not a documented event.)

### 10. A Brief History of Time  
**Boost (+1 fields)**

His 1988 book explains the universe to everyone and sells over 25 million copies. A scientist who cannot speak aloud becomes one of the best-known voices on Earth.

### 11. Paralympics, 2012  
**Story**

Stephen speaks at the opening of the London Paralympics. He urges people to look up at the stars and not down at their feet, and to be curious. He also speaks for access to healthcare and technology for disabled people.

### 12. 2018: Westminster Abbey  
**Story**

Stephen Hawking dies on 14 March 2018, aged 76, 55 years after the doctors' first forecast. His ashes are placed in Westminster Abbey near Newton and Darwin. His work belongs to a whole team of people.
